The rules

What Nevada requires on a regulated trade's website

  • HVAC — NRS 624.720(3). All advertising by a licensed contractor must include the company name and the contractor's license number. The board names the website explicitly among the places it must appear. The license number belongs on the site, not only on the contact page. Missing it is a violation, not a style choice.
  • HVAC — NRS 624.720(7)(a). Advertising is defined broadly and names the Internet and directory listings under a contractor heading. Directory profiles count as advertising, so the license number has to be consistent across them.
  • HVAC — NRS 624.720(3). It is unlawful for a licensed contractor to publish a false or misleading statement of material fact intended to induce someone to use their services. Claims about speed, savings or results need to be supportable.
  • HVAC — NSCB FAQ Central. Work performed by a heating or air conditioning contractor requires a license regardless of the small-repair threshold. An HVAC site cannot lean on the handyman exemption framing.
  • Contracting — NRS 624.720. Same license number requirement, same broad definition of advertising, same prohibition on false or misleading statements. Identical treatment to HVAC; both trades sit under the same board.
  • Dental — NAC 631.190. The Board issues specialty licences in only twelve areas: oral and maxillofacial pathology, oral and maxillofacial surgery, orthodontia, periodontia, prosthodontia, pediatric dentistry, endodontia, public health, oral and maxillofacial radiology, dental anesthesiology, oral medicine, and orofacial pain. This list decides whether a service page or heading implies specialist status. Anything outside it cannot be called a specialty at all.
  • Dental — NAC 631 table of contents. Chapter 631 of NAC contains a section titled False or misleading advertising at NAC 631.270 and an unprofessional conduct section at NAC 631.230. Chapter revision date shown as 6/7/2026. Confirms the citations are current and correctly numbered. Subsection text still needs retrieving before any rule from it is published.
  • Plumbing — NRS 624.720(3). All advertising by a licensed contractor must include the company name and the contractor's license number, and the board names the website among the places it must appear. Same rule as HVAC and contracting; all three sit under the Nevada State Contractors Board.
  • Plumbing — NRS 624.720(7)(a). Advertising is defined broadly and names the Internet and directory listings under a contractor heading. Directory profiles count as advertising, so the license number has to match across them.
  • Plumbing — NRS 624.720(3). It is unlawful for a licensed contractor to publish a false or misleading statement of material fact intended to induce someone to use their services. Response-time and savings claims need to be supportable.
  • Plumbing — NSCB FAQ Central. Plumbing work requires a license regardless of the small-repair threshold that exempts minor work. A plumbing site cannot use handyman framing.

Sources: official board page, official statute, official, text read in full from the official chapter page and official, section titles confirmed; subsection text not retrieved, retrieved 2026-09-18. Cited as supplied by the source named, not independently re-verified against the statute.
